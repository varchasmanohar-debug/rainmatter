Initial Thoughts

  • Around 68% of the portfolio (28 of 41 companies) are B2C consumer brands. The remaining 32% are B2B ingredient, infrastructure, or corporate plays.
  • 29% of all companies sit in the food and nutrition cluster, making it the single most concentrated sub-sector. Within that, 3 companies (Arboreal, Devigere Bio, TWF Flours) are pure B2B ingredient suppliers; the remaining 9 sell directly to consumers.
  • The Track and Monitor column is the thinnest at 3 companies, all B2C, anchored by Ultrahuman at the premium end. The diagnostics infrastructure layer (Adsys, AI Health Highway, Niramai, Blackfrog) sits entirely in B2B with no affordable consumer monitoring equivalent alongside it.

Daily Supplements
Zero companies

The gap here is not protein. Rainmatter already has protein covered directionally through Trunativ, Superyou, and several B2B ingredient plays. The gap is the daily supplement stack that every urban Indian 25-40 is buying monthly: multivitamins, omega-3, vitamin D, magnesium, iron, and electrolytes. These are health staples, not fitness-specific products, which means the market is far broader than sports nutrition. The users of Fittr, Devils Circuit, and Yoska buy electrolytes and vitamin D every month. None of it flows to a Rainmatter-backed brand.

Activewear and Performance Apparel
Zero companies

110 million Indians participated in fitness and sports in 2024. Every one of them buys clothing for it. The market today is Decathlon at the value end and Nike or Puma at premium, with almost nothing in between that has personality. Indian gym culture has its own aesthetic now: printed tank tops, oversized gym tees, specific cuts of joggers. Activewear has become identity wear. The brands that will win are the ones that understand Indian gym culture specifically, not adapted global sportswear. BlissClub proved this works for women. The men's equivalent barely exists. E-commerce sports apparel grew 35% year-on-year on Myntra and Amazon in 2024, and the portfolio has zero position in any of it.

Gut Health and Probiotics
No consumer brand

India's probiotics market nearly doubled to Rs 2,070 crore in 2025. The category is crossing into sports nutrition with probiotic protein powders and recovery blends. Arboreal and Devigere are ingredient plays, not consumer brands. Investors at Fireside Ventures call it the "next big bet" in Indian healthcare. There is no consumer-facing gut health or probiotic brand across 41 companies. The Good Bug went from seed to Series B in under three years on the back of repeat purchase, not just awareness. That trajectory tells you the consumer behaviour is already converted.

Boutique Fitness Formats
Underrepresented

HIIT, pilates, reformer yoga, and MMA studios are the fastest-growing segment in Indian fitness at 18.8% CAGR through 2030 per Deloitte. This is also the highest-AOV format in fitness. Sol Pilates in Bengaluru charges Rs 2,000 per reformer session. A 24-session pack is Rs 27,000. Members are spending Rs 15,000 to Rs 27,000 per membership cycle, recurring. That is a higher AOV than almost any food or supplement brand in the portfolio. Value gyms dominate by volume, but boutique is where premium urban spending concentrates. Devils Circuit and Sisters in Sweat are adjacent. No pilates, HIIT, or boutique yoga brand exists in the portfolio.

Metabolic Health and GLP-1 Adjacency
Critical gap

Mounjaro launched in India in 2025. India has 77 million diabetics and the third-highest obesity rate globally. GLP-1 drugs will reshape how a large consumer segment buys food, supplements, and fitness services. At least 8 portfolio companies are directly exposed: The Whole Truth, NOTO, Lo! Foods, Fittr, Trunativ, Ultrahuman, Arboreal, Evolved Foods. None of them appear to have a response strategy. No brief exists on how their market changes. This is the most time-sensitive gap in the portfolio right now.

AI Coaching and Performance Intelligence
Emerging gap

Every serious runner or gym-goer in India now has a Garmin, a Whoop, or an Ultrahuman ring generating hundreds of data points daily. Garmin tells you your cadence is 162 steps per minute and your oscillation is 8.4cm. It does not tell you what to do with that information. A Garmin paired with HRV data, sleep data, and two weeks of training history should be able to say: drop intensity on Thursday, do mobility work, you are under-recovered. That synthesis does not exist as a product. The data layer is built. The intelligence layer on top of it is missing. Ultrahuman is already in the portfolio generating CGM and ring data. Fittr coaches nutrition. Yoska coaches endurance athletes. An AI coaching layer that sits on top of wearable data and connects all three is the product nobody in India has built yet.

Brands already in these spaces.
What they tell us about each category.

For each gap, these are the brands actively building in India right now. The purpose is not to say Rainmatter should back all of them. It's to understand the category through what founders are already building, what's working, what the consumer behaviour looks like, and where real whitespace still exists within each gap.

01 / Pattern recognition across data
Built a tickerization system linking 1.3M consumer panel transactions to stock tickers, then backtested it against actual company financials to prove predictive power. The work required holding two things simultaneously: understanding what the data could do technically, and understanding what a hedge fund needed to trust it commercially. That is exactly the skill a health and fitness analyst needs: mapping what the market data says to what a portfolio decision should be.
02 / Root cause before recommendation
Three weeks into my internship I traced client data complaints to a root cause in the email capture pipeline and redesigned it at source, freeing up 30-40% of the data team's time. The same instinct applies to portfolio analysis: the nutrition cluster concentration is not a problem to react to, it's a pattern to understand before drawing a conclusion about whether it's intentional or not.
03 / Built the sales pipeline from zero
Grew Vumonic's pipeline from zero to 1,000+ deals and 2,000+ clients in a two-person team. This required understanding what clients actually needed (often different from what they asked for), pricing the data product correctly, and closing without a sales playbook. Deal sourcing for a fund is structurally similar: you need to find founders before they are obvious, understand what they're building before a deck exists, and develop a view independently.
