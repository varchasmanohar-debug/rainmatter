03 / Gaps

Five categories. Fast growth.
Wide open for the right backer.

Indian consumer spending in these five categories is growing at 8 to 19 percent annually. Each one has a clear connection to what Rainmatter has already built in the portfolio. None of them have meaningful coverage yet.

Gap
Why it matters to this portfolio
Market signal
Daily Supplements
Zero companies

The gap here is not protein. Rainmatter already has protein covered directionally through Trunativ, Superyou, and several B2B ingredient plays. The gap is the daily supplement stack that every urban Indian 25-40 is buying monthly: multivitamins, omega-3, vitamin D, magnesium, iron, and electrolytes. These are health staples, not fitness-specific products, which means the market is far broader than sports nutrition. The users of Fittr, Devils Circuit, and Yoska buy electrolytes and vitamin D every month. None of it flows to a Rainmatter-backed brand.

Activewear and Performance Apparel
Zero companies

110 million Indians participated in fitness and sports in 2024. Every one of them buys clothing for it. The market today is Decathlon at the value end and Nike or Puma at premium, with almost nothing in between that has personality. Indian gym culture has its own aesthetic now: printed tank tops, oversized gym tees, specific cuts of joggers. Activewear has become identity wear. The brands that will win are the ones that understand Indian gym culture specifically, not adapted global sportswear. BlissClub proved this works for women. The men's equivalent barely exists. E-commerce sports apparel grew 35% year-on-year on Myntra and Amazon in 2024, and the portfolio has zero position in any of it.

Gut Health and Probiotics
No consumer brand

India's probiotics market nearly doubled to Rs 2,070 crore in 2025. The category is crossing into sports nutrition with probiotic protein powders and recovery blends. Arboreal and Devigere are ingredient plays, not consumer brands. Investors at Fireside Ventures call it the "next big bet" in Indian healthcare. There is no consumer-facing gut health or probiotic brand across 41 companies. The Good Bug went from seed to Series B in under three years on the back of repeat purchase, not just awareness. That trajectory tells you the consumer behaviour is already converted.

Boutique Fitness Formats
Underrepresented

HIIT, pilates, reformer yoga, and MMA studios are the fastest-growing segment in Indian fitness at 18.8% CAGR through 2030 per Deloitte. This is also the highest-AOV format in fitness. Sol Pilates in Bengaluru charges Rs 2,000 per reformer session. A 24-session pack is Rs 27,000. Members are spending Rs 15,000 to Rs 27,000 per membership cycle, recurring. That is a higher AOV than almost any food or supplement brand in the portfolio. Value gyms dominate by volume, but boutique is where premium urban spending concentrates. Devils Circuit and Sisters in Sweat are adjacent. No pilates, HIIT, or boutique yoga brand exists in the portfolio.

Metabolic Health and GLP-1 Adjacency
Critical gap

Mounjaro launched in India in 2025. India has 77 million diabetics and the third-highest obesity rate globally. GLP-1 drugs will reshape how a large consumer segment buys food, supplements, and fitness services. At least 8 portfolio companies are directly exposed: The Whole Truth, NOTO, Lo! Foods, Fittr, Trunativ, Ultrahuman, Arboreal, Evolved Foods. None of them appear to have a response strategy. No brief exists on how their market changes. This is the most time-sensitive gap in the portfolio right now.

AI Coaching and Performance Intelligence
Emerging gap

Every serious runner or gym-goer in India now has a Garmin, a Whoop, or an Ultrahuman ring generating hundreds of data points daily. Garmin tells you your cadence is 162 steps per minute and your oscillation is 8.4cm. It does not tell you what to do with that information. A Garmin paired with HRV data, sleep data, and two weeks of training history should be able to say: drop intensity on Thursday, do mobility work, you are under-recovered. That synthesis does not exist as a product. The data layer is built. The intelligence layer on top of it is missing. Ultrahuman is already in the portfolio generating CGM and ring data. Fittr coaches nutrition. Yoska coaches endurance athletes. An AI coaching layer that sits on top of wearable data and connects all three is the product nobody in India has built yet.

04 / Who's Building in the Gaps

Brands already in these spaces.
What they tell us about each category.

For each gap, these are the brands actively building in India right now. The purpose is not to say Rainmatter should back all of them. It's to understand the category through what founders are already building, what's working, what the consumer behaviour looks like, and where real whitespace still exists within each gap.

Daily Supplements Multivitamins, omega-3, vitamin D, magnesium, electrolytes
Wellbeing Nutrition Science-backed supplements in melts, patches, and powders. HUL-backed. Rs 170cr revenue FY25 with 25% coming from offline retail, which signals the category is moving beyond D2C into mainstream distribution. Clean-label positioning across vitamins, gut health, collagen, and plant protein. The HUL investment is the most telling signal: a large FMCG with deep pharmacy and supermarket reach bought in because they see the daily supplement shelf shifting from Himalaya and Amway to new-age brands. Series B / HUL
Patch Up Health Transdermal vitamin and mineral patches. Founded July 2024. Rs 4.2cr revenue in year one with 50,000+ users. The format innovation here is worth noting: patches solve the gut absorption problem that standard supplements have, which is a legitimate clinical differentiator. Shark Tank appearance gave national visibility. The brand is early enough that the founding team is still making every product decision, which is exactly where Rainmatter prefers to enter. Angel / Early 2024
Nutrabay Multi-brand sports nutrition platform plus private label. 1.5M customers. 80% YoY growth FY24. Raised $5M from RPSG Capital. The platform model is interesting because it solves India's counterfeit supplement problem by curating authenticated products, and the private label builds margin on top of distribution. RPSG's managing partner noted demand growing "beyond metros," which confirms the category is Tier-2 ready. Series A / RPSG Capital
Plix Plant-based supplements across weight management, skin, hair, and daily wellness. Marico invested Rs 369cr for majority ownership, valuing the brand at roughly Rs 635cr. That valuation on a supplements brand signals how aggressively large FMCGs are moving to own this shelf. The Marico play with Plix mirrors the HUL play with OZiva: both are incumbents buying category positions they couldn't build organically. Majority / Marico
Activewear and Performance Apparel Identity-driven, Indian gym culture aesthetic, prints, cuts, community
BlissClub Women's activewear built community-first. Bengaluru. Backed by Matrix and Elevation Capital. The brand's growth came not from paid ads but from building a community of women who wanted activewear that reflected their identity, not just their sport. That is the model. The fact that no equivalent exists for men in India is a visible gap. Indian gym culture for men has a strong aesthetic (tank tops, joggers, specific fits) that global brands do not serve. Series A / Matrix, Elevation
Terractive Performance activewear brand. Backed by DeVC in 2024. Early stage, founder-led. Growing through D2C and offline fitness communities. Still pre-scale, which makes it the most relevant comparison point for what a Rainmatter entry looks like: a brand with strong community fit, early revenue, and a clear cultural positioning before institutional capital takes over. Angel / DeVC 2024
Agilitas Sports Sportswear for athletes and sports organisations. Raised Rs 530cr. Targeting the organised sports segment rather than gym culture. Useful as a market reference point: the fact that Rs 530cr has gone into the organised sports apparel space confirms investor conviction in the category, even if Agilitas itself is targeting a different buyer than what the portfolio needs. Series A+ / Rs 530cr
Dida Indian performance sportswear for runners and gym-goers. D2C-first. Bootstrapped. Strong word-of-mouth in running communities. The bootstrapped status here is a signal worth noting: the brand is growing organically through community, which is exactly how activewear with cultural identity works. Paid acquisition does not build identity brands. Community does. Bootstrapped
Gut Health and Probiotics Synbiotics, microbiome testing, fermented products, probiotic nutrition
The Good Bug Synbiotics, probiotics, kombucha, kefir. Founded 2022. Raised Rs 100cr in May 2025 led by Susquehanna Asia VC and Fireside. The trajectory is the most important data point here: seed to Series B in under three years, on repeat purchase, not just awareness. That means consumers are coming back monthly. Gut health is not a one-time purchase. The category behaves like a subscription, which is a business model Rainmatter should be interested in. Series B / Fireside, Susquehanna
Sova Health Gut microbiome testing plus personalised nutrition plans. Science-first positioning. Interesting because it sits one layer above product: the test tells you what your gut needs, then the brand sells you what fixes it. That closed loop is harder to build but has significantly higher retention than a supplement brand with no diagnostics layer underneath. Seed
Peping India's first hyper-personalised probiotic via gut testing. Axilor-backed. Launched June 2024. Sits at the intersection of diagnostics and nutrition, which are two areas Rainmatter already has positions in. A portfolio company or investment here could theoretically have synergies with Niramai (diagnostics) and the nutrition cluster simultaneously. Seed / Axilor
Cosmix Wellness Plant-based adaptogens and superfoods. Bengaluru. Rs 30cr revenue FY24. Acquired by Marico in February 2026 for $24.9M. The Marico acquisition is the most useful signal in this category: a 6x+ revenue multiple on a supplements brand with no profitability requirement tells you how strategically FMCG incumbents value this shelf position. That is the exit market for a Rainmatter-backed brand in this space. Acquired / Marico Feb 2026
Boutique Fitness Formats Pilates, HIIT, reformer yoga, MMA: high-AOV, recurring, community-driven
Sarva Yoga Yoga and mindfulness studio chain plus digital platform. 100+ centres. Raised $9M+. Shilpa Shetty-backed. The scale here shows the format can go beyond one or two premium locations. Sarva built a franchise model that maintains quality across cities, which is the hard problem in boutique fitness. Worth studying as a template for how a national boutique brand gets built in India. Series A+
Reform Athletica Reformer pilates studio. Mumbai and Bengaluru. Bootstrapped with strong waitlists. The waitlist dynamic is the most important data point here. Waitlists in boutique fitness signal demand that outstrips supply, which is the exact moment to back a brand. At Sol Pilates in Bengaluru, a 24-session reformer pack is Rs 27,000. That recurring spend from a loyal member base is a fundamentally different business model from a gym membership. Bootstrapped
Shyft Yoga, therapy, and mental wellness combining ancient practice and modern science. Digital-first. The digital delivery model is the differentiation from Sarva. Shyft is building the same consumer but without the real estate overhead, which changes the unit economics significantly. Worth tracking as a counter-model to the physical studio rollout. Seed
Fitso Sports and fitness access platform connecting users to pools, courts, and studios. Zomato-backed, now operates independently. Strong Tier-1 coverage. Fitso solves the discovery and booking layer, not the studio itself. It's the aggregator play in boutique fitness. Relevant because an aggregator captures category growth without picking a single format winner. Series A / Zomato-backed
Metabolic Health and GLP-1 Adjacency PCOS, diabetes management, weight coaching, GLP-1 support programmes
Veera Health PCOS and metabolic health platform for women. Backed by Surge (Sequoia), Y Combinator, Global Founders Capital. 110 million Indian women have PCOS. The founders came from McKinsey and Yale, which gives the clinical credibility that a metabolic health brand needs to convert a sceptical consumer who has already tried and failed with other solutions. The PCOS angle is directly adjacent to the GLP-1 wave: PCOS drives insulin resistance, which is exactly what GLP-1 drugs treat. Seed / Surge, YC
Laiqa Wellness Hormonal health tracking and holistic wellness for women. Cycle-synced care model integrating Ayurveda and modern science. Raised Rs 15cr seed in 2024 from IvyCap. The cycle-synced model is the differentiation: instead of generic supplements, Laiqa changes the product recommendation based on where the user is in her cycle. That personalisation layer is what separates it from a standard women's wellness brand. Seed / IvyCap 2024
Fitterfly Digital therapeutics for diabetes, PCOS, and weight management. Clinically rigorous. Raised $18.9M across four rounds before being acquired by PB Healthcare (PolicyBazaar parent) in November 2025. The acquisition by a large insurance-adjacent group is telling: PB Healthcare sees metabolic health coaching as a natural extension of health insurance, which is the distribution model that makes metabolic health platforms scale beyond direct-to-consumer. Acquired / PB Healthcare 2025
Fitelo Dietician-led nutrition coaching with 800+ certified dieticians and 10M+ clients. Covers PCOS, diabetes, thyroid management. Offers a 5-year post-programme warranty, which is an unusual retention mechanism that signals high confidence in outcomes. The dietician model at scale is essentially a services business with brand attached, which has different margin dynamics than a product brand but very high consumer trust. Series A
AI Coaching and Performance Intelligence Cross-device data synthesis, actionable daily recommendations, personalised training intelligence
Whoop Coach (global reference) Whoop launched an AI coaching layer on top of their recovery and strain data in 2024. It answers questions like "why is my recovery low" and "what should I do today" in plain language. But it only works within the Whoop ecosystem. There is no product that does this across Garmin, Ultrahuman, Apple Watch, and Strava simultaneously, which is where most Indian athletes actually live. The cross-device aggregator version is the unbuilt product. Global / Not in India
Future (global reference) US-based personal training app that pairs each user with a real coach who reviews wearable data and sends daily workout plans. Raised $75M. The insight is that consumers do not want more data dashboards. They want someone to read the data for them and tell them what to do. Future does this with human coaches. The AI version of Future, at Indian price points and across Indian fitness contexts, does not exist yet. Global / Not in India
Portfolio synergy angle This gap is particularly relevant to Rainmatter because of what already exists in the portfolio. Ultrahuman generates ring and CGM data. Fittr coaches nutrition and has community scale. Yoska coaches endurance athletes. An AI coaching product that ingests Ultrahuman data, understands a user's Fittr nutrition context, and gives a daily training recommendation is not a hypothetical. It is a product that three portfolio companies are already generating the inputs for. Portfolio opportunity
What the Indian founder would build The Indian version of this is not a premium subscription at Rs 5,000 a month. It's a WhatsApp-native coach that reads your Garmin sync, asks you three questions in the morning, and tells you what to do in Hindi or English. The form factor is the differentiator. The data layer already exists. The intelligence and delivery layer does not. This is the whitespace an Indian founder with fitness domain expertise and engineering skill could own. Unbuilt

Built a tickerization system linking 1.3M consumer panel transactions to stock tickers, then backtested it against actual company financials to prove predictive power. The work required holding two things simultaneously: understanding what the data could do technically, and understanding what a hedge fund needed to trust it commercially. Three weeks into my internship I traced client data complaints to a root cause in the email capture pipeline and redesigned it at source, freeing up 30-40% of the data team's time. The same instinct applies to portfolio analysis: the nutrition cluster concentration is not a problem to react to, it's a pattern to understand before drawing a conclusion about whether it's intentional or not.
03 / Built the sales pipeline from zero
Grew Vumonic's pipeline from zero to 1,000+ deals and 2,000+ clients in a two-person team. This required understanding what clients actually needed (often different from what they asked for), pricing the data product correctly, and closing without a sales playbook.
